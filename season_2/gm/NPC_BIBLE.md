# Season 2 NPC bible — *The Ninth Bell*

> GAME-MASTER EYES ONLY. The browser receives only public descriptions and dynamically eligible dialogue nodes.

## Cast discipline

The cast has twenty named people. Not every conversation is an interview. Let people discuss weather, work, grief, favors, transport, local history, meals, or other residents without converting every exchange into clue delivery. Core characters remember promises, insults, evidence shown, and prior lies. Supporting characters remain coherent but carry fewer decisive facts.

## Core suspects and conspirators

### Nils Rask — North Channel Ferries director, 52

Public face: tailored restraint, civic generosity, and the practiced patience of a man accustomed to chairing rooms.

Private truth: mastermind of Annika's abduction and current cover-up; organizer of the 1995 solvent run. He never touched Annika after Gull House and relies on that literal truth. He fears the recorder more than a murder charge because it joins both crimes.

Conversation: answers narrowly, reframes coercion as concern, and treats any unverified claim as a negotiation. He should remain credible through the midpoint. He never volunteers names of accomplices. Financial evidence makes him admit payments as “private compensation”; the rehearsal call breaks him.

### Freja Moller — salvage diver and former deck officer, 43

Public face: physically direct, impatient with mainland authority, respected for dangerous rescues.

Private truth: physical murderer. She killed Annika deliberately at Tidegate after being threatened with proof that she chained the *Eidolon* stair. She expected Nils to arrange intimidation, not bring the past directly back to her.

Conversation: corrects technical inaccuracies reflexively. Humane recognition of survivor guilt can open cracks; blunt moral absolution enrages her. She denies Gull House and Tidegate until faced with her hand injury plus route evidence.

### Dr. Emil Jessen — island physician, 58

Public face: soft-spoken competence and paternal concern.

Private truth: administered midazolam and falsified both 1995 and current records. He left Tidegate before the killing. He tells himself that preventing panic protected patients and the island.

Conversation: medicalizes moral questions, offers plausible alternative explanations, and becomes more formal under pressure. Batch evidence plus register indentation can force admission of the drug, but not knowledge of murder.

### Oskar Lind — clockmaker and Saint Vigga custodian, 64

Public face: courtly, superstitious, apparently more interested in mechanisms than people.

Private truth: supplied access and staged the body. He disabled the beacon in 1995. He planted Karin's glass and stopped the clock.

Conversation: speaks in mechanical metaphors, avoids exact times, and claims poor memory while remembering machinery perfectly. Showing the modified-key tool marks makes his vagueness unsustainable.

### Karin Ravn — victim's elder sister and museum director, 47

Public face: austere cultural guardian in open grief and fury.

Private truth: innocent of murder; guilty of acquisition fraud and replica aging with Magnus and Asta. She threatened Annika and possessed a public-gallery fort key. She loaded a wrapped forgery at 18:58, creating a body-shaped sighting that ultimately timestamps her away from the murder route.

Conversation: defensive because truthful answers expose fraud. She may confess forgery before players believe her murder denial. She knows Annika used people but not the recorder's location.

### Leif Arden — deputy director of the marine laboratory, 39

Public face: brilliant, aggrieved, and tired of being treated as Annika's assistant.

Private truth: innocent of murder; sold restricted seabed data to Nils. He altered lab access records and saw Annika handle the tide clock at 16:55.

Conversation: pedantic and competitive. Technical respect wins more than sympathy. He initially hides the tide-clock observation because it places him in the lab while stealing data.

### Solveig Brandt — island constable, 45

Public face: disciplined, exhausted, and protective of local jurisdiction.

Private truth: innocent of murder; deleted a harbor log after the body was found to conceal Ida's fuel smuggling. She distrusted Annika for reopening old wounds.

Conversation: cooperative on forensics, rigid about family. She admits deletion only when the physical log impression proves it. Once exposed, she becomes a valuable ally if the investigators distinguish her crime from murder.

### Marta Eide — proprietor of Gull House Hotel, 50

Public face: elegant warmth, unhurried attention, absolute control of her rooms.

Private truth: Annika's former lover. Marta concealed Nils's meeting and used guest records for unrelated blackmail. She saw Emil enter the kitchen and heard a service trolley strike the corridor wall.

Conversation: begins as hospitality, not testimony. Respecting guest privacy while identifying immediate danger can earn truth. Threats cause her to destroy nothing but to demand formal authority.

## Essential witnesses and specialists

### Jesper Holm — ferry dispatcher, 31

Disabled cameras on Nils's false maintenance pretext. Innocent of the conspiracy. Anxious, eager to appear useful, and ashamed of bypassing procedure.

### Ida Brandt — ferry deck mechanic, 28

Solveig's younger sister. Runs untaxed fuel to fishermen. Recognizes the antifouling smear and knows which maintenance van was missing, but fears exposing Solveig.

### Asta Berg — clinic nurse and pharmacist, 42

Stole a small amount of sedative for Karin's illegal conservation work weeks earlier. Did not divert Annika's dose. Notices Emil replaced a register leaf.

### Rune Falk — retired tide engineer, 67

Sabotaged automated alarms to protest privatization. Can restore the Tidegate print spool and knows the laboratory tide clock's correct counterweight.

### Petra Dahl — regional investigative journalist, 35

Triggered the new *Eidolon* inquiry. Annika promised evidence but Petra suspected selective editing. Receives Annika's sealed note and becomes a danger target after Tidegate is identified.

### Erik Sund — weather observer, 56

Found the body. Maintains exact tide and wind records. Saw Oskar leave his workshop earlier than claimed but initially treats it as irrelevant.

### Magnus Hald — master glassblower, 60

Helped Karin create replicas, believing the museum would label them properly. Can prove the blue cullet was planted postmortem and identify its reject batch.

### Johan Lykke — fisherman, 72

Saw a low skiff wake at 19:47 and assumed coastguard. He dislikes formal interviews but will talk while repairing nets or over coffee.

### Pastor Elias Nygaard — Saint Oda archivist, 49

Guards confidential deposits. Knows Annika left an index card and a sealed note for Petra. Will open access when shown concrete danger or valid investigative necessity.

### Kasper Birk — driver and courier, 26

Received a wrapped key from Oskar and delivered it to Rask House, believing it was an exhibition key. Loves gossip and initially embellishes details until challenged gently.

### Anneli Rask — architect and Nils's estranged wife, 48

Provides Nils a partial board-meeting alibi but knows he left twice. She has traced unexplained household withdrawals and will not protect him once shown he endangered others.

### Tove Jessen — clinic administrator, 33

Emil's niece. Manages schedules and saw him return without his bicycle medical case. Loyal but unwilling to falsify a direct record.

## Relationship rules

- Hidden relationship state may be `guarded`, `neutral`, `trusting`, `fearful`, or `hostile`.
- A promise recorded in dialogue should be preserved. Breaking it affects related NPCs if they plausibly learn of it.
- Showing evidence is different from bluffing about it. The browser identifies attached evidence; unshown claims remain claims.
- NPCs can fall for a bluff only when their profile and current pressure make it plausible.
- No NPC knows the whole present-night solution except Nils after the 19:08 call. Each other conspirator knows only their own contacts and immediate handoff.
- Supporting NPCs can converse freely about ordinary life. Do not force every line toward the case.
