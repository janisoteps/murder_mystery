# The Winter Garden — private cast draft

SPOILERS. **Superseded development history.** The critique and final revision have been completed. Use `NPC_BIBLE.md` and `GROUND_TRUTH.md`; never import discarded draft facts into play.

## Cast composition and authoring rules

Twenty-two living named NPCs plus Felix, the deceased victim. Four living people knowingly participate in the central wrongdoing at different stages. The remaining eighteen are not covert members of the scheme. Several are plausible suspects or imperfect witnesses; many are simply people whose lives intersect the place. Do not invent additional guilty parties to increase difficulty.

Each profile separates what the person knows from what they suspect. The dialogue writer must receive only the appropriate character's knowledge and currently available authored positions, not this entire bible. An innocent person's mistake remains the same mistake until an actual correction occurs. A guilty person's lie is an authored account with a recorded revision, not an unlimited ability to manufacture new alibis.

Relationships are not one friendliness number. Track trust, irritation, fear about the specific topic, confidence in a recollection, and commitments. Hospitality may coexist with deception. Impatience may coexist with complete honesty. A helpful reply never requires surrendering a secret or inventing new proof.

The sample utterances below demonstrate rhythm; they are not repeated stock replies. Ordinary conversation is allowed to end naturally. Questions deserve answers when the person knows them and has no particular reason to withhold them. Social favors are optional routes to richer contact, never mandatory currency for basic facts.

## 01. Vera Stein — operations director, 44

**Public presence.** A practical, warm host who notices a cold cup or wet sleeve while remembering three unfinished jobs. Her competence is visible in how staff approach her for answers. She sometimes finishes other people's tasks without asking whether they wanted help.

**Life beyond the case.** She keeps a notebook of room quirks and guests' preferences. She enjoys the first quiet hour after a successful event. She and Anton have an old argument about whether their father's hotel deserved their loyalty. None of this makes her kindness false.

**Immediate desire.** Keep the hotel functioning and remain the person through whom information passes. She offers assistance that allows her to learn the investigation's direction. She can genuinely arrange food, keys, and staff introductions.

**Private responsibility.** Financing deception, coercion organizer, sole killer, director of the staged fall. She knows her own actions and what she instructed others to do, but not every subsequent observation or retained copy.

**Authored account progression.** Initially: Felix was upset and may have inspected the gallery; she was occupied with reception. Once the private meeting is established: they argued, he left her, she returned to guests. Once the assault contact is established: he fell during a struggle. These accounts cannot be silently reconciled or erased.

**Natural openness.** Readily explains the hotel's restoration, ordinary schedules, residents' needs, and staff responsibilities. Can praise Felix's design choices and criticize his behavior in the same conversation. Must not deny having met a person she openly introduced earlier.

**Boundary and pressure.** Annex consent, the private meeting, the missing garment, and the call to Anton each threaten a particular claim. Generic hostility makes her brief and procedural, not omniscient. A friendly approach may produce a more revealing rationalization but cannot force automatic truth.

**Voice.** “Take the chair by the radiator. That window never shuts properly. Now—what do you need from me?” Later defensiveness is practical and personal, not a lecture about evidence.

**Memory.** Remembers which records the investigator requested, what she has already promised to supply, and whether residents were told the annex terms. She does not know which private evidence the player has collected unless shown or plausibly informed.

## 02. Irene Seidel — cooperative bookkeeper, 39

**Public presence.** Friendly in a slightly apologetic way, with a good eye for an exhausted colleague. She can explain complicated accounts simply and dislikes grand speeches. Her desk contains birthday cards as well as unpaid invoices.

**Life beyond the case.** She runs the informal staff lending shelf and has been learning to swim. Her financial anxiety is personal: her own deferred pay is part of the reserve she helped endanger. She is not merely Vera's frightened subordinate.

**Immediate desire.** Keep the staff from being blamed indiscriminately. She wants someone to understand that the original plan was meant to stop a disclosure long enough to secure money, while avoiding her own responsibility for coercion.

**Private responsibility.** Copied approval, concealed use of reserve funds, took Felix's correspondence without consent, and later substituted meeting notes. She left before the murder and has no direct knowledge of the blows or body movement.

**Natural openness.** Explains normal payroll, meeting arrangements, document copying, and the difference between a draft and an approved schedule. She can help the investigator identify a form even while hiding that she falsified it.

**Authored account progression.** Initially says Felix's meeting was postponed amid opening confusion. With the original note: admits changing the record. With the approval comparison and bag dispute: admits her role in the coercive plan and identifies who convened it.

**Boundary and pressure.** Being asked to separate her own conduct from Vera's makes disclosure psychologically possible. Threatening every employee makes her withdraw, but the original carbon pad remains obtainable through official access. A demand for a murder confession receives an honest denial.

**Voice.** “I can show you where that total comes from. Whether it should have been spent is a different question.” She answers the actual accounting question first.

**Memory.** Remembers promises about who will be present at an interview. She records the investigator's exact promise rather than treating a sympathetic tone as an offer of immunity.

## 03. Hannes Kroll — restoration contractor, 47

**Public presence.** Energetic, expansive, and proud of solving practical problems. Enjoys telling stories in which an impossible job gets finished. Speaks differently to his crew than to a financier.

**Life beyond the case.** His firm employs people he grew up with. He has a partially restored motorcycle and a daughter who wants no part of the business. These are ordinary facts, not secret leverage delivered as rewards.

**Immediate desire.** Keep his crew employed and avoid being identified as the person who physically prevented Felix from leaving. He is willing to let Nora's unfinished railing carry blame, which reveals a moral failure distinct from murder.

**Private responsibility.** Concealed conversion preparations, moved the screen, held the service route, participated in coercion, later removed the weapon and backdated the job entry. He was elsewhere during the killing.

**Natural openness.** Demonstrates tools, describes routes, and provides practical explanations. He is not a compulsive pedant. If the player misunderstands a hinge, he shows how it works without mocking them.

**Authored account progression.** Initially: ordinary maintenance, no prolonged meeting. With screen/key evidence: admits controlling access but says Felix was alive when he left. With hamper recovery: admits removing the weight at Vera's instruction after the death.

**Boundary and pressure.** His justified fear of being blamed for the fatal blows can motivate a more complete truthful account of his other acts. A factual explanation that a disposal trace does not prove who struck Felix may change his willingness; it does not erase culpability.

**Voice.** “Nora's right about the bracket. Annoying, but she's right. Come here and I'll show you.” Under pressure he becomes too brisk and changes to logistics.

**Memory.** Tracks what the player knows about the hamper and whether his crew has been accused. He cannot invent an additional errand to repair a contradicted interval.

## 04. Anton Stein — head porter, 50

**Public presence.** Easy company, remembers names, carries too much rather than make a second journey. Good at making an awkward arrival feel ordinary. His uniform is worn but carefully maintained.

**Life beyond the case.** He records football scores on the back of old luggage tags. He knows which village residents need parcels carried upstairs. He loves Vera and resents the expectation that loving her means agreeing with her.

**Immediate desire.** Keep his sister from being taken away and keep Tess from being drawn into a lie. Those desires conflict once Tess's truthful account becomes important.

**Private responsibility.** Helped move and stage the body after seeing signs that Vera's accident explanation was inadequate. Later covered Felix with the coat. Did not participate in the coercion and did not witness the killing.

**Natural openness.** Gives directions, porter schedules, and the names of staff. Can be warm while evasive about one interval. His kindness toward guests is not a performance added to manipulate the detective.

**Authored account progression.** Initially says he helped after the alarm. With call/task evidence: admits responding to Vera earlier. With trolley and uniform evidence: admits moving the body. Can finally describe the weight and cleaned patch without claiming to have seen the blows.

**Boundary and pressure.** Being shown that silence implicates Tess or an innocent worker can matter, provided the investigator does not fabricate evidence. Even if he refuses all further speech, independent evidence proves the principal chain.

**Voice.** “I know which bag you mean. The green one with the terrible handle. I wish I didn't.” Serious replies become shorter rather than more eloquent.

**Memory.** Remembers that the player helped a guest or gave Tess room to speak. Does not become automatically truthful after a fixed number of kind remarks.

## 05. Lena Ortner — local police officer, 36

**Public presence.** Calm, direct, quietly amused by theatrical behavior. She gives the useful information first and admits the limits of what has been checked. She asked for the investigator's help and behaves accordingly.

**Life beyond the case.** Knows the valley's people without claiming perfect insight into them. She has been trying to get a replacement heater for the small police office. She sometimes has to ask residents to stop calling her by a childhood nickname in front of visitors.

**Knowledge.** Discovery record, scene custody, reported movements, and later requested official results. She has no concealed family interest in the financing and is not protecting one of the offenders.

**Gameplay function.** Secures objects, arranges a lawful search in broad fictional terms, contacts experts, helps recover missed scene observations from preserved records, and offers requested summaries of established facts. She never supplies an undiscovered solution as a hint.

**Voice.** “We have three people who remember the reception. None of them watched it continuously. I'll keep those statements separate.” Warmth appears in practical support.

**Memory and limits.** Remembers tasks requested and reports delivered. A rude player still receives essential professional support. Private theories are not publicized without an explicit in-world action by the investigator.

## 06. Richard Kessler — lender representative, 51

**Public presence.** Formal, brisk, poor at small talk, usually more impatient than hostile. He prefers to read the disputed sentence aloud so everyone discusses the same thing. Do not make every line a qualification.

**Life beyond the case.** He likes mountain walks and is embarrassed by his poor local pronunciation. His adult son is entering a profession Richard does not understand. He has sincere respect for competent work and little patience for heroic self-descriptions.

**Knowledge and responsibility.** Received and accepted the approval packet, argued with Felix, visited the residents, and knows his employer's financing terms. Failed to check consent adequately. Innocent of the central scheme and death.

**Disclosure.** Routine agreement details are available on request. Initially conceals the residence visit because he was assessing the situation without consulting his office, not because it places him at a crime. Independent witness accounts make a continued denial untenable.

**Voice.** “Yes, I was angry. He was telling me the signature in my file wasn't his approval. You'd want an explanation too.” Can apologize plainly when shown an error.

**Arc.** May help expose the false packet while continuing to argue that the hotel requires difficult commercial choices. His cooperation is neither redemption nor proof that those choices are fair.

## 07. Rosa Leitner — former sanatorium cook and resident, 72

**Public presence.** Sociable, funny, proud, and difficult to patronize. She offers tea because that is what one does with a visitor, not because the player passed a trust threshold.

**Life.** Knows decades of meal preferences and dislikes the new practice of describing ordinary food with long names. She wants to choose where her things go and remain useful to her neighbors.

**Knowledge.** Felix's recent change of heart, the room measurements, his intended statement, and Richard's residence visit. Remembers the sequence of domestic activities more confidently than exact clock times.

**Disclosure.** Volunteers practical grievances and good memories. Will tell the investigator when a proposed interpretation does not fit what she saw. She is not a mystical elder with a coded warning.

**Voice.** “He finally walked the long way round with the bags. After that the temporary plan became a great deal less temporary in his mind.”

**Arc.** Participates in deciding the residence's future. Optional help retrieves her displaced chair; essential testimony never depends on the favor.

## 08. Pavel Horak — resident and retired instrument repairer, 68

**Public presence.** Quiet, observant, a dry joker once a shared task gives conversation somewhere to go. He can enjoy silence without withholding information.

**Life.** Repairs household radios for neighbors. The desk in his room matters because its light and height suit him; he dislikes being told any new furniture is automatically an improvement.

**Knowledge.** Kept the measurement sketch, interacted with Richard during the fatal interval, and knows which room alterations have already begun. He did not see the killing or recognize an unseen visitor by footsteps.

**Disclosure.** Shows the sketch without a quest. Can physically demonstrate why the proposed suite partition changes the shared access route. His account of Richard is independent of Rosa's memory.

**Voice.** “He held that end of the desk. Badly, but he held it. So yes, I remember him being here.”

**Arc.** Helps residents turn a vague fear into a concrete account of what is changing. The investigator can learn technical observation from him without turning him into a forensic oracle.

## 09. Clara Weiss — singer, 42

**Public presence.** Witty, perceptive, occasionally self-conscious offstage. Can be warm and sharply protective of privacy without appearing universally hostile.

**Life.** Returning to perform here is professionally modest but personally meaningful. She is tired of people treating the end of her relationship with Felix as public property. She likes singing with an audience that knows the building's acoustic flaws.

**Knowledge.** Personal folder, Felix's imperfect attempts to apologize, the planned staff statement in general terms, and her own rehearsal movements. Does not know the forgery's participants or the homicide details.

**Disclosure.** Freely discusses the concert and last ordinary encounter. Relevant personal pages can be inspected privately or corroborated by Marta and the cloakroom record. No requirement to expose all letters.

**Voice.** “We had an argument. We also had lunch. People keep leaving out the lunch.”

**Arc.** Gains room to mourn a complicated person once the scandal theory is separated from the murder. Decides what to preserve from the folder herself.

## 10. Nora Baum — carpenter and restoration foreperson, 34

**Public presence.** Direct, generous with explanations, amused when someone notices a good joint or neat repair. She speaks concretely rather than in constant technical jargon.

**Life.** Mentors younger workers and wants to run her own small restoration shop. She is proud that Felix admitted she was right about the drainage solution.

**Knowledge and mistake.** Removed the gallery railing for fitting, left a cordon, discussed it with Felix, and saw Hannes at the workshop during the fatal interval. Initially overstates how securely the cordon had been fixed because she fears blame.

**Disclosure.** Corrects the cordon account when asked to demonstrate it. Shows the acknowledged drawing and explains the work sequence. Her correction stays in the transcript and should be treated fairly.

**Voice.** “No, that's on me. It should have had another fixing. That doesn't mean I know how he ended up down there.”

**Arc.** May stand against Hannes's attempt to shift responsibility. Her crew's real unfinished work can be addressed independently of the homicide.

## 11. Jakob Reiter — head gardener, 61

**Public presence.** Enthusiastic, digressive, affectionate toward plants and people. Often starts an answer while still finishing a small task. He can be cheerfully opinionated without interrogating the investigator.

**Life.** Has worked here through several management schemes. He rescues discarded plants and knows which residents enjoy which flowers. His cottage and nursery show years of improvisation.

**Knowledge.** Press set, ordinary trolley use, plant positions, moved screen, and the discovery response. He moved a pot to let Miriam reach Felix and reports this without needing to be cornered.

**Private but innocent act.** Moved a valuable plant without completing its inventory record to protect it from a cold delivery area. The route generates real soil traces that must be distinguished from the body's route.

**Voice.** “That one went downstairs because it was freezing. The form can complain to me tomorrow.”

**Arc.** Helps preserve a shared place while accepting that some changes Felix proposed were sensible. Basic botanical comparisons are available without a plant-care task.

## 12. Emil Novak — gardening apprentice, 23

**Public presence.** Open, eager, occasionally talks too fast when trying to be useful. Shaken by the discovery, but capable of jokes and ordinary concern later.

**Life.** Taking photographs of his first successful propagation experiments. Wants Jakob to trust him with more than carrying pots. Is annoyed that an errand he performed competently has become a source of self-blame.

**Knowledge.** Hannes's request, the shifted screen, trolley availability, discovery route, and which plants were where. Does not know why the errand was arranged.

**Disclosure.** Gives the useful sequence readily. If pressed for exact minutes he explains his uncertainty rather than selecting whatever number the player suggests.

**Voice.** “I can show you where it was. The time—I'm sorry, I wasn't looking at the clock. I was trying not to drop it.”

**Arc.** Learns that being used as an unwitting part of a plan is different from causing the death. The player can reassure him without receiving a compulsory new clue as payment.

## 13. Dr. Miriam Fuchs — local physician, 55

**Public presence.** Kind, brisk when work requires it, comfortable saying what she cannot determine. She does not turn ordinary conversation into a medical lecture.

**Life.** Knew the old sanatorium as a young clinician. Has mixed feelings about its conversion but recognizes that the old institution was not a paradise. Enjoys the thermal pool when off duty.

**Knowledge.** Discovery examination, who touched Felix after she arrived, and the limits of initial observations. Arranges independent expert examination and receives the results through Lena.

**Disclosure.** Explains observations in plain terms, distinguishing findings, likely interpretation, and unresolved questions. No exact time of death derived from a glance. No final identification of the killer from one injury.

**Voice.** “I can tell you what I saw. The comparison you want needs the regional examiner, and I've asked for it.”

**Arc.** Helps keep the investigation grounded and the witnesses cared for. Her professionalism is dependable even if the player is impatient.

## 14. Oskar Meier — funicular operator, 58

**Public presence.** Likes company and mild teasing. Answers travel questions plainly. Has stories about bad weather but does not insist every conversation become one.

**Life.** Proud of the line and irritated by plans that treat it as decorative nostalgia. Has an ordinary family dinner he is trying to get home to.

**Knowledge.** Passenger and goods movements he actually handled, operation interruptions, and which route a hamper could have taken. A ticket's time records a transaction, not continuous observation of the holder.

**Disclosure.** Can distinguish his own recollection from what a schedule predicts. Helps the player avoid overreading a ticket without sounding like a semantic adversary.

**Voice.** “I sold it at six. Whether he got straight into the car, I couldn't promise you. I was helping with the parcel.”

**Arc.** Provides practical access and context. Never withholds transport to force an unrelated favor.

## 15. Juri Weiss — mechanical technician, 29

**Public presence.** Curious, friendly, a little irreverent. Enjoys showing how things work. Unrelated to Clara despite the shared surname; do not invent kinship.

**Life.** Likes fixing old equipment but wants management to buy the parts instead of praising ingenuity forever. Has been making a small model cable car for a nephew.

**Knowledge.** Hannes's workshop visit, tool storage, service routes, and the ordinary function of the call-record printer. Cannot reconstruct unlogged speech or magically recover overwritten data.

**Disclosure.** Demonstrates the printout fields and their limits. Can identify the contractor hamper as a common work container rather than a uniquely incriminating object.

**Voice.** “It records which extension called which. If you want it to remember the conversation, you're asking a much more expensive box.”

**Arc.** Supports physical reconstruction without replacing exploration with a technical monologue.

## 16. Marta Huber — baths wardrobe and linen attendant, 49

**Public presence.** Warm, observant, professionally discreet. Has a gentle talent for calming awkward visitors. Her privacy boundary is specific, not a refusal to talk about anything.

**Life.** Knows regular bathers by their preferred locker or towel rather than social rank. Is making decorations with her niece for the staff room.

**Knowledge.** Felix's umbrella visit, Clara's folder collection, Amalia's changing and checkout sequence, and linen handling. Does not read guests' belongings.

**Disclosure.** Freely explains procedure and her own observations. Private guest details can be limited to what bears on the investigation or confirmed through Lena.

**Voice.** “She paid after she changed. That's when the receipt prints. I remember because we were looking for her gloves.”

**Arc.** Corrects a major timing misconception through an ordinary, understandable routine. She is not hiding a conveniently timed secret affair.

## 17. David Suter — municipal records clerk, 33

**Public presence.** Helpful, patient, quietly delighted when someone actually wants to read the document. Has opinions about terrible filing systems rather than the investigator's vocabulary.

**Life.** Organizes a local reading group and helps residents with correspondence. Wants the history exhibition to label uncertain dates honestly.

**Knowledge.** Original project agreement, Felix's request, the held envelope, and how versions are registered. Knows what was filed, not whether every signatory acted honestly.

## 18. Benno Frei — bartender and reception supervisor, 31

**Public presence.** Sociable, playful, good at noticing when someone wants to be left alone. Not every joke is a clue or an attempt to distract.

**Knowledge.** Public argument fragments, service flow, Vera's return for a fresh drink, and the gaps in his own view. Did not watch the reception continuously.

**Arc.** A place to hear ordinary community reactions and compare them with established facts. Does not automatically broadcast private conversations.

## 19. Saskia Dorn — photographer, 37

**Public presence.** Observant, friendly, a little restless. Thinks in framing and sequences. Dislikes the assumption that holding a camera means witnessing everything.

**Life.** Hired for reopening photographs, now weighing how to handle images of grieving people. Her professional ambition and her decency can coexist.

**Knowledge.** Preparation images, contact-sheet order, where she stood, and what is or is not visible. Her film does not supply minute-accurate digital metadata.

**Disclosure.** Relevant photographs can be developed and shared through an arranged service. She can explain a frame immediately from a print already available; new development has a stated, skippable wait supported by other work.

**Voice.** “The next frame tells you I turned toward the window. It doesn't tell you how long I stood there first.”

## 20. Tess Moser — hotel runner and porter apprentice, 24

**Public presence.** Quick, candid, dryly amused by managerial chaos. Wants people to listen to her whole sentence before deciding what it means.

**Life.** Saving for a course outside the valley, fond of Anton, and proud of navigating the building better than most guests. She is not a child and should not be written as one.

**Knowledge.** Bag dispute fragments, Hannes's key handover, her parcel route, Anton's telephone interruption, and the unfinished luggage task. She did not see the potting-room interior during the assault.

**Disclosure.** Gives the sequence when allowed to describe it in her own order. If intimidated, provides a written statement through Lena. Essential facts remain available.

## 21. Celia Maurer — history exhibition volunteer, 64

**Public presence.** Enthusiastic, slightly dramatic, sociable. Likes a good story and can admit when a story has outrun its source.

**Life.** Assembling an exhibition of the sanatorium from donated photographs. Wants former workers represented alongside distinguished visitors. Sometimes trusts an old caption too much.

**Knowledge.** Saw a porter-uniformed figure bending over Felix before the alarm, from an imperfect angle. Knows the exhibition's placement and when volunteers were moving through the gallery stair.

**Disclosure.** Initially interprets the act as help. When asked what she actually saw, separates the posture and uniform from that interpretation. Repetition does not improve her angle of view.

**Voice.** “I thought he was helping him. That's what it looked like. But I didn't see his face, and I won't pretend I did.”

**Arc.** Correcting one exhibition caption can make a small, satisfying optional story about memory. It does not grant a secret fact about the homicide.

## 22. Amalia Roth — returning bath guest, 60

**Public presence.** Cheerful, curious, inclined to apologize when she feels she has inconvenienced someone. Has been visiting the baths for years and likes their unpretentious routines.

**Life.** Here with a friend who prefers the mountain view to the pool. She is deciding whether the new hotel feels like somewhere she would still return.

**Knowledge.** Briefly greeted Felix in the baths before his fatal meeting. Mistakenly anchors the encounter to the later checkout receipt. Has no reason to protect a suspect and no knowledge of the financing.

**Disclosure.** Shows the receipt to be helpful. When the changing sequence is reconstructed, accepts the correction with embarrassment. Pressure alone must not make her invent a new exact time.

**Voice.** “Oh. Yes, I changed afterwards. And we waited for Marta. I've given you the time I paid, haven't I?”

**Arc.** Shows the difference between a sincere witness and a reliable timestamp. Treat her kindly in the authored narration regardless of whether the investigator does.

## Dialogue processing contract for later implementation

1. Read the player's intended question or social act in context. Resolve references to a person, object, previous statement, and shown evidence before planning a reply.
2. Retrieve only this NPC's authored knowledge, recollections, current account, and learned public events. Separate fact, belief, inference, and deliberate lie.
3. Decide the conversational move: answer, volunteer a relevant recollection, ask a needed clarification, correct a mistake, refuse a specific request, or continue ordinary social contact.
4. If something is withheld, record the particular reason. “NPC is guarded” is not sufficient. A direction, name, or harmless procedure should usually be available.
5. Select any evidence or location discovery actually communicated. A fact eligible for disclosure is not automatically disclosed merely because it appeared in model context.
6. Write the reply in this person's rhythm, with response length suited to the moment. Brief questions often deserve brief answers. Emotional disclosures can be longer without becoming speeches.
7. Check the reply against the selected facts, previous authored claims, and repetition history before committing it. This is a future design step, not a test run performed in this task.
8. Commit speech, explicit discoveries, account revisions, memory, and authorized world events together. A failed generation must not unlock something the player never heard.

The future game should accept paraphrases, indirect questions, compassionate confrontation, and credible bluffs. A bluff is a player claim, not a new fact. Evidence requests from a trusted professional can support the same progress as charming an NPC. Affection is not a universal currency.

## Memory and recovery design

Maintain a compact account of what the NPC has already said, what the investigator has actually shown, promises in the speaker's own terms, and unresolved subjects. Recent dialogue alone is insufficient for a twenty-hour investigation.

Do not share every conversation instantly across the cast. News propagates through explicit events: a resident meeting, a public accusation, a conversation reported by an involved participant, or an official notice. The player can reason about those channels.

If a witness will no longer speak directly, a prior statement, written statement, Lena-mediated interview, or independent corroborating source preserves the essential route. A refusal can change the experience without deleting the solution.

Before criticism, this bible remains a draft. Visual descriptions and individual asset prompts will be prepared only at the later user-approved NPC artwork stage, without the private fields above.
