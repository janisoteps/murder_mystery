# The Winter Garden — expanded private story draft

## 1. What this story is about

The public premise is a death during the reopening of an Alpine sanatorium as a thermal hotel. The underlying conflict is ownership of a place that several people have spent their lives sustaining. An institution can be financially rescued and still cease to belong to the people for whom it mattered. The crime comes from particular choices inside that conflict, not an abstract curse, hereditary villainy, or a hidden old massacre.

The investigator should discover a community before discovering a conspiracy. Someone needs a parcel collected. A singer rehearses badly, laughs, and starts again. A porter knows which visitor cannot manage the stairs. A resident insists that the best chair belongs where it has always stood. The people responsible for the crime also inhabit this ordinary life; the innocent people have personalities beyond their usefulness to a murder inquiry.

The case contains one current murder, coordinated wrongdoing before it, knowing assistance after it, and several unrelated acts that become misleading in the investigation. These are different acts with different participants and levels of knowledge. The player must distinguish them rather than assign everyone standing near a secret to a single undifferentiated conspiracy.

The story's emotional question is whether doing indispensable work for others gives someone the right to decide for them. Its investigative question is what happened between a private meeting and a public discovery, and which apparently connected actions belong to the same event. The material evidence should let the player answer without accepting a confession.

## 2. Place and occasion

Bad Auersee is a fictional German-speaking settlement in an Alpine valley. The date is Friday, 27 November 1998. Country-specific laws are not puzzle rules; legal process is handled in general terms by the local officer. The season is grounded in period objects: paper duplicates, internal telephones, keys, film cameras, cassette playback, mechanical equipment, and a limited number of office computers. These objects have ordinary uses, not a universal secret mechanism.

The Sonnenfels sanatorium stands above the village. Its large glass winter garden joins the old residential east wing, the main hotel, and the thermal complex through enclosed and open paths. The buildings are a mixture of restrained nineteenth-century public architecture, additions made when the sanatorium expanded, and conspicuous new hotel fittings. The reopened hotel is neither a ruin nor a flawless luxury resort. Staff are still finding places to store things.

A funicular serves the upper terraces. The lower station opens onto the village square; workshop and goods facilities adjoin the upper station. Roads remain available to official vehicles. Snow changes the atmosphere and makes some outdoor routes unpleasant, but never conveniently severs all communications or forces an unexplained three-day police absence.

The reopening weekend includes an evening reception, a small concert, public tours, and an exhibition of sanatorium photographs. Former workers and residents have been invited. Guests expect thermal baths and mountain views. The staff cooperative expects to see the result of years of deferred wages and repair work. A lender's representative expects approved paperwork and a viable business.

The east residence still contains occupied staff flats and a shared winter room. Access to the shared room, therapy pool sessions, and the upper terrace has been part of village life for decades. Those rights exist in the story's written project agreement; the mystery does not require outside knowledge of property law. People disagree about how the new business can afford them.

## 3. The victim as a living person

Felix Arendt, 46, is the restoration architect and an independent signatory for completion of specified works. He is exact about space and often careless about how his corrections make people feel. He remembers a person's preferred chair more readily than their birthday. He sketches while listening, which some people experience as attention and others as condescension.

Felix fought to preserve the winter garden, but he also signed a temporary circulation plan that made the east residents take a long service route during construction. He treated that inconvenience as a technical interim state. Rosa Leitner forced him to walk it with her shopping bags. His later change of mind matters because it was earned and incomplete, not proof that he had always understood everything.

He and singer Clara Weiss had a relationship that ended without a crime. She dislikes his habit of turning shared experiences into architectural stories in which he is the perceptive observer. They recently began speaking civilly again. He asked her to keep a folder of old sketches and letters away from the opening exhibition, where an enthusiastic curator had offered to display them.

Felix is bad at apologizing spontaneously. He leaves corrected plans, pays a debt, or repairs a thing instead. In the residence, a seat has been adjusted to Rosa's preferred height. In the workshop, Nora has a plan bearing his handwritten admission that her drainage solution was better. These discoveries make the death personal without adding secret crimes to his biography.

On the day of his death, Felix intends to withdraw an approval he did not actually give and explain a concealed change to the residents and staff. He also intends to acknowledge his real earlier negligence about their access. His documents do not contain a complete list of villains, a coded last testament, or a prediction of his own murder.

## 4. The financial and human pressure

Vera Stein, 44, the hotel operations director, has made herself indispensable. She arranged temporary wages, kept suppliers from walking away, and found rooms for residents during repairs. Many people's gratitude is justified. Her mistake is treating that gratitude as permission to bind them to decisions they have not made.

Six weeks before opening, a funding shortfall threatened to stop the work. Vera accepted a revised financing package which converted the east residence into recoverable commercial space if the hotel failed an early payment condition. The staff cooperative had approved borrowing against hotel revenue, not occupied flats or the shared winter room. A plain-language schedule in the fictional contract spells out the difference.

Vera told herself she could refinance before the conversion clause mattered. She authorized furnishing surveys of the residence as if the change had already been approved. The scheme is therefore visible in rooms and routes as well as in paperwork: suite measurements, new lock allocations, incompatible seating plans, and residents receiving misleading notices about temporary storage.

Bookkeeper Irene Seidel, 39, helped assemble the misleading approval packet. She copied Felix's signature from a genuine earlier approval sheet onto a new annex schedule. She knew the schedule had not been approved. Her justification was that the signatures would catch up when the opening succeeded. She also concealed a commitment of the cooperative's reserve funds. Irene is responsible for these acts; she was not an unwitting typist.

Restoration contractor Hannes Kroll, 47, accepted payment for annex conversion preparation while continuing to describe the work to his crew as temporary hotel storage. He knew residents had not consented. His company was close to failure, and several employees had advanced money for materials. His complicity is concrete but does not make every worker in his company guilty.

Richard Kessler, 51, represents the regional lender. He signed off on a packet he should have checked more carefully and has an incentive to minimize the embarrassment. He did not arrange the forgery, detention, or killing. He is capable of cold decisions, but the game must not turn his profession or unpleasant manner into proof of murder.

Felix notices that a furniture drawing refers to his supposed final annex approval. He requests the underlying schedule. Richard's office sends a copy on the afternoon of the opening. Felix recognizes his genuine signature attached to a proposal he rejected. He gathers the earlier plan, his carbon correspondence, and the resident-access agreement. He intends to speak at a staff meeting after the reception.

## 5. Who does what

Vera organizes an attempt to seize Felix's documents and keep him away from the staff meeting. Irene and Hannes knowingly assist that coercive plan. None of the three is simply arranging an innocent conversation. They intend to make Felix sign a withdrawal of his objections, or to hold him while they recover the originals and tell the staff he has left.

Irene is to intercept the briefcase and locate the annex correspondence. Hannes is to close the public service approach under cover of a minor maintenance job and prevent Felix from walking out through the potting-room vestibule. Vera is to negotiate privately and obtain his signature. Their plan is improvised and morally serious, but it does not require a sophisticated intelligence operation.

Vera alone commits the homicide. At the fatal moment neither Irene nor Hannes is in the potting room. Felix refuses to retract his objections. Vera deliberately strikes him with an iron botanical press weight, then strikes again when he tries to rise and catches her sleeve. Her later description of one unfortunate fall is false. The distinction between a loss of temper and deliberate continuation matters to her characterization, not as a legal riddle the player must classify.

Anton Stein, 50, Vera's older brother and the hotel porter, arrives after the killing. Vera initially tells him Felix has fallen during an argument. Anton sees enough to recognize that her account does not explain the scene. He nevertheless helps move the body. His first act is partly disbelief and loyalty; his continued staging is a knowing choice.

Hannes subsequently removes the contaminated press weight in a tool hamper and falsifies the time of the supposed maintenance job. He now understands that a death is being concealed. Irene, after learning of the discovery, substitutes a version of the staff meeting notes that represents Felix as having accepted a postponement. Neither is transformed retroactively into an eyewitness to the killing.

No innocent witness secretly knows the complete solution. Anton can identify what Vera told him and what he saw after arrival; he cannot quote the fatal argument. Hannes can identify the coercion and his disposal route, not the blow. Irene can prove the paperwork and detention plan, not which object hit Felix. The player must combine their partial accounts with independent material evidence.

## 6. Fixed geography of the crime

The winter garden is one map destination containing a public entrance court, central palm hall, upper gallery, potting room, delivery vestibule, and an exterior sheltered path. The potting room is on the same level as the palm hall. A wheeled plant trolley can pass through the delivery vestibule to the palm hall without stairs. No secret passage is added later.

The upper gallery overlooks part of the palm hall but not the potting room. A short section of gallery railing has been taken down by Nora Baum's carpentry crew for fitting. The work area was cordoned off. Its absence provides a convincing accident story at a glance, especially because Felix is known to inspect unfinished work.

Hannes moves a work screen to close the normal sightline through the delivery vestibule. He also relocates the maintenance sign from the actual gallery work to the service approach. Both are ordinary movable objects with documented prior positions. The closure creates opportunity; it is not an electronic lock that can be hacked by guessing a code.

The potting room has a broad plan chest, a potting bench, a botanical press, and a washing area. The iron press weights are portable tools already used by the gardener. The weapon's identity is established by several observations and an expert examination, not a magical unique wound shape.

Vera and Anton move Felix on the plant trolley through the vestibule into the palm hall. They put him beneath the gallery opening, dislodge a planter, and arrange a shoe and the coat to suggest a fall. They do not make the scene perfect. The audience must not need exact physics to notice that the scene warrants examination.

Anton returns briefly after Vera leaves. He straightens Felix's collar and places the coat across his chest. This changes what is visible between an initial glimpse and the first official photograph. It explains a witness discrepancy without moving the time of death, adding a second victim, or making the witnesses irrational.

## 7. Fixed chronology before the killing

Times below are authoring truth. Individual clocks and witnesses have stated precision; do not present all these times as available facts. When the player cannot resolve a minute, accept the correct interval.

**15:10–16:00.** Richard receives the requested copy of the approval packet at the hotel office. Irene notices the fax and tells Vera Felix is asking for supporting pages. Staff are arranging flowers, lamps, and chairs. Photographer Saskia Dorn takes informal preparation pictures, including the botanical press and the service screen in their ordinary positions.

**16:20.** Felix obtains the packet. He recognizes the false annex approval. He asks municipal records clerk David Suter to hold a copy of the earlier access agreement for collection. David confirms that the office will close to visitors but that the reading-room return locker can hold the envelope. It is labeled as normal correspondence, not hidden by a riddle.

**16:45.** Felix goes to the residence and discusses the measured rooms with Rosa and resident Pavel Horak. He promises to explain the change at the staff meeting. Rosa asks him to admit his own part in making their old entrance unusable. Felix says he will. Pavel keeps a room measurement sketch because he wants his desk put back in the right place.

**17:05.** Nora discusses the gallery railing with Felix in the workshop. She tells him it must remain cordoned until tomorrow. He initials her drawing to acknowledge the unfinished work. This contradicts the later suggestion that he wandered unknowingly into an unmarked hazard, but does not by itself rule out a fall.

**17:25.** Clara collects Felix's personal blue folder from the hotel cloakroom with his permission. Wardrobe attendant Marta Huber sees them exchange a few strained but affectionate words. The folder's removal later looks like evidence theft to people who have heard only that Felix had important papers.

**17:50–18:00.** Felix and Richard argue in a corridor about the annex approval. Richard insists that his office has a signed schedule. Felix insists it is not his approval. Bartender Benno Frei hears anger and the word “signature,” not a threat to kill. Richard goes to the residence to check whether the residents knew about the conversion.

**18:08.** Vera meets Irene and Hannes in the hotel service office. They agree to intercept Felix, recover the briefcase, and obtain a postponement. Vera says the staff meeting must not happen with those papers on the table. This conversation is not recorded. Its existence can be reconstructed from movements, a handwritten instruction slip, and the participants' admissions if obtained.

**18:18.** Hannes asks Emily Novak to take a plant delivery down to the lower vestibule and moves the work screen. Emily is not instructed to lie and does not know the purpose of the errand. Hannes takes a potting-room key from the working key board, leaving the normal tag rather than a forged entry.

**18:24–18:28.** Felix stops at the baths to speak to Marta about the staff meeting and retrieve a borrowed umbrella. Guest Amalia Roth sees him and exchanges a greeting. She later associates this encounter with her bath payment receipt, but that receipt is printed at 18:52 when she checks out, after changing and waiting. The time error is hers, not a hacked clock.

**18:31.** Felix enters the delivery vestibule for the meeting Vera requested. Hannes prevents him from leaving when Irene takes the briefcase to the adjacent service desk. Felix protests. Hotel runner Tess Moser hears a man's demand to return a bag. She sees only portions of the group through the screen and cannot identify every speaker.

**18:34–18:37.** Irene removes the annex correspondence from the briefcase, leaves personal and ordinary site papers, and carries the selected pages to the hotel office. She believes Vera and Hannes will pressure Felix into a postponement. She knows she has taken papers without his consent. Her sleeve catches a torn appointment slip beneath the desk blotter; the retained portion later corroborates the meeting's purpose and route.

**18:38–18:41.** Hannes leaves the vestibule after Vera tells him she can finish the discussion. Tess sees him pass a key to Vera but cannot identify the key by its small silhouette. Hannes proceeds to the workshop and is seen there by Nora and mechanic Juri Weiss. Their testimony gives him an interval alibi for the blows, not absolution for the detention.

## 8. Killing, staging, and discovery

**18:43–18:46.** Felix tells Vera he will not pretend the residents agreed. Vera takes a press weight from the open botanical press and strikes him as he turns toward his recovered briefcase. He catches the sleeve of her cardigan as he attempts to stand; she strikes again. He dies in the potting room. A piece of dark wool remains in his hand. His blood is on the weight, the plan-chest edge, and a cloth Vera uses to wipe the surface.

**18:47.** Vera calls the porter desk on the internal telephone. Anton receives the call. The switchboard record, where available, establishes a connection between two extensions and a duration, not the words or the speaker's identity. Porter apprentice Tess remembers Anton taking the call and abandoning a luggage task.

**18:50–18:53.** Anton enters the potting room. Vera calls the death an accident. Anton sees the weight and the cleaned patch, which make that explanation doubtful. He initially proposes summoning the doctor. Vera tells him the financing deception will bring down the hotel and makes him responsible for what happens to the staff if he calls. His decision to help is his own.

**18:54–19:01.** They use the plant trolley to move Felix to the palm hall. One wheel has a damaged rubber segment that produces an interrupted deposit through spilled potting grit. This is route evidence, not an exact tire fingerprint identifying its pusher. Anton dislodges a planter from a low stand; Vera puts Felix's shoe near the landing area and shifts the cordon.

**18:57–18:59.** On his way back from the workshop, Hannes sees the trolley being returned and Vera's stained wiping cloth. She orders him to remove the weight and make the maintenance record agree with her account. He puts the wrapped weight in a tool hamper and carries it to the thermal service area, where contractor tools are being washed and stored. He later claims he had left the winter garden before the argument.

**19:02.** Vera returns to the reception rooms. Benno remembers pouring her a fresh drink. Several guests remember her general presence over the evening, but none can honestly place her continuously in the reception between 18:30 and 19:00. Her alibi is social familiarity mistaken for continuous observation, not a recording of a speech or an impersonator.

**19:04–19:06.** Anton returns to Felix, straightens his collar, and covers the upper chest with the coat. From the gallery stair, exhibition volunteer Celia Maurer sees someone bending over the body. She recognizes the porter uniform, not the face. She is initially unsure whether she saw an attempted rescue. Her observation is valuable without becoming a secret eyewitness to murder.

**19:10–19:12.** Emily returns for the plant trolley and finds the body from the opposite approach. She shouts. Jakob Reiter and Dr. Miriam Fuchs arrive, followed by Vera. Miriam checks Felix and requests that the scene be left alone. Jakob moves a pot aside to let her kneel and later admits it plainly; this innocent disturbance must be recorded.

**19:14–19:25.** Irene, hearing of the death, substitutes a meeting-note page saying that Felix accepted a postponement. She retains the original in a routine carbon-copy pad rather than destroying it. Hannes backdates his job entry. These acts create evidence of coordinated concealment without requiring perfect coordination or a meeting after the murder.

**19:30 onward.** Lena Ortner secures the winter garden, records who has entered, and requests an outside investigator and regional examination support. She does not permit the reopening to proceed through the scene. People gather in other rooms, eat, telephone relatives, and begin forming explanations. Their rumors are not authorial truth.

**20:05.** The player-controlled investigator arrives at the winter garden entrance. Only this map location is accessible. Lena offers a concise handover, asks what the investigator wants preserved, and gives practical assistance. The investigator is never assigned dialogue, emotions, or a hidden personal connection to the victim.

## 9. What the opening lets the player do

The first scene is a place, not a dossier dump. The player can orient themselves in the palm hall, look up toward the gallery, inspect the edge of the cordon, talk with Lena, and meet Emily or Jakob without demanding testimony. These actions establish geography and human stakes before the first theory.

Photographs, a doctor-requested examination, the displaced planter, and the marked trolley route each create a legitimate line of inquiry. The player may discover the potting room immediately. Do not hide it until an act number is reached. It is part of the visible architecture, and an observant player is entitled to inspect it.

The body examination provides observations and bounded conclusions through a professional report. The player does not need to diagnose injuries from pixels. Early findings say the proposed fall is doubtful and the body may have been moved. Later comparison can strengthen the homicide reconstruction; it must not suddenly introduce an invisible fatal toxin.

The location unlocks initially come from actual leads: a work label or Nora's name opens the workshop route; a service delivery tag or staff directions opens the hotel; a bath laundry label or a witness's account opens the thermal complex. A player who talks can reach the same essential destinations as one who examines objects carefully.

No character makes the investigator earn basic directions through flattery. Closed rooms inside a known location can require permission, a relevant discovery, or Lena's assistance, but public geography is not confidential. Newly unlocked locations show the reason for the lead without describing what will be found there.

## 10. The first plausible explanation: a disastrous inspection

Nora had removed a railing. Felix inspected unfinished work. A planter is broken below the gallery. Hannes's maintenance record places work in that area. These facts make an accidental fall plausible and also raise the possibility that a worker moved things after the accident to conceal negligence.

Nora is frightened that a correct admission will cost her crew their livelihood. She initially describes the barrier as more secure than she left it. Under patient clarification she corrects herself. The game should treat that correction as meaningful honesty, not proof that every later statement is false.

The theory fails through independent observations: the potting-room trace; movement of the plant trolley; the relationship between the body, clothing, and supposed landing point; and the professional examination. The gallery can have been unsafe without being the murder scene. The player earns progress by separating an actual safety failure from the cause of death.

If the player rejects the fall in the first hour, the story advances naturally. Nobody is required to preserve this explanation until a midpoint. It remains relevant as the cover story chosen by the perpetrators and as a source of Nora's misleading behavior.

## 11. The second plausible explanation: the lender's confrontation

Richard had a public argument with Felix about a signature. His office held the changed agreement. He used the residence entrance without announcing the visit to reception. An early witness places a formally dressed visitor on a path that also serves the winter garden. Taken together, these observations justify investigating him.

Richard does not respond well to vague accusations. He does answer direct factual questions and will show ordinary correspondence. His embarrassment concerns his reliance on the submitted packet and his unauthorized effort to assess the residents' opposition before reporting to his superior. He is not constitutionally unable to cooperate.

At the residence, Pavel remembers Richard helping move a heavy desk, and Rosa remembers arguing with him about exactly what the agreement says. Their independent activities overlap the fatal interval. A telephone connection made from the residence office and a neighbor's observation support the visit. No single stamped receipt is treated as an unbreakable personal alibi.

Excluding Richard as the physical killer does not erase his role in accepting and defending an exploitative arrangement. He can become a source of evidence against the deception while remaining a person whose preferred solution the residents reject. This is moral complexity rather than a second secret murder plot.

## 12. The third plausible explanation: a personal betrayal

Clara's blue folder has disappeared from the cloakroom. Felix's last personal notes mention an apology. Someone heard an argument about what he had a right to make public. A player can reasonably wonder whether the planned disclosure was intimate rather than financial.

The folder contains letters, sketches, and an unfinished exhibition caption. Clara took it before the coercive meeting, with permission. Marta witnessed the handover, a cloakroom record supports it, and a preparation photograph places the folder under Clara's arm. The dates and content establish that this is a different set of papers from the annex packet.

Clara will not hand over every intimate letter to satisfy an unsupported theory. She can show the folder's contents privately to Lena, let the investigator inspect relevant dated pages, or describe the agreed collection while Marta corroborates it. Respect for privacy changes the form of proof, not whether the main case is solvable.

Resolving this branch reveals Felix's imperfect humanity and can make Clara an ally. She is particularly good at distinguishing a remembered person's habitual manner from a rumor about their last words. She cannot use that skill to identify a voice she did not hear.

## 13. Reconstructing the coordinated coercion

Once the player follows the briefcase and service route, several apparently mundane preparations align: the relocated screen, Emily's errand, the key handover, the torn appointment slip, and selected missing pages. These establish an organized meeting that was not as voluntary as Vera initially describes.

The people who left that meeting have different reasons to lie. Irene fears the copied approval and theft of papers. Hannes fears the physical coercion and later disposal. Vera needs the meeting itself to disappear. Their accounts do not become identical merely because they are accomplices.

Hannes can truthfully say Felix was alive when he left. Irene can truthfully say she never saw a weapon used. Those facts matter. The investigator should not have to accept their preferred conclusion that truthful denials of one act excuse every other act. Conversely, suspicion of one act must not authorize the game to invent another.

Tess provides an independent account of the bag dispute and later key handover. Her route is established by the parcel she delivered and the person who received it. She cannot be made to recognize the key or repeat a conversation that the screen prevented her hearing. The case is stronger because her limits remain stable under pressure.

The planner's control emerges from converging instructions and benefits, not a convenient list of conspirators. Vera directs the meeting, receives the key, retains Felix after others leave, summons Anton, and organizes the public explanation. Each point has its own source and its own uncertainty.

## 14. Establishing the murder and its author

The potting room contains local traces inconsistent with a harmless meeting: a cleaned patch on the plan chest, a trace beneath its lip, the disturbed botanical press, and floor deposits leading to the trolley. The first official photograph preserves the dark textile in Felix's hand before later handling. The professional report treats the injuries as an assault, with limitations stated in ordinary language.

The recovered press weight carries material linked to the victim. Its felt backing and chipped coating connect it to the missing member of the botanical press set. This establishes a likely weapon and a disposal chain. It does not prove that whoever last carried the hamper struck Felix.

Vera's damaged cardigan provides an edge comparison with the textile retained in Felix's hand. The garment is collected through a justified search, voluntary surrender, or Anton's specific information followed by official recovery. It is not found in an arbitrary locked drawer only after every side quest is complete.

The textile link places Vera in close physical contact. She may claim she touched Felix while helping at discovery. The preserved scene photograph, Miriam's account of who touched the body, and the garment's location and condition let the player assess that claim. No piece of evidence alone reads as “Vera is the murderer.”

The decisive reconstruction combines the assault location, the interval when Vera remained with Felix, the defensive contact, the call to Anton, and the effort to stage a fall. The motive documents explain the pressure but are not a substitute for proving the act. Hannes's or Anton's confession can clarify the chain; neither is mandatory.

## 15. The body mover is not automatically the killer

Celia's observation of a porter bending over Felix initially raises a direct suspicion against Anton. His route, uniform, access, and evasive account warrant investigation. The case should let the player make that inference and then inspect its limits rather than having a narrator instantly correct it.

Tess's luggage task and the telephone connection establish why and when Anton left his post. He was engaged elsewhere during the earlier coercion and fatal interval. Multiple people interacted with him there, including a guest whose luggage complaint is independently documented. The alibi is an overlapping sequence of ordinary events rather than one precise witness timestamp.

The trolley and his clothes establish movement after the killing. His knowledge of the potting-room scene is incriminating for concealment. His return to straighten the coat explains a discrepancy between Celia's glimpse and the official scene without requiring a second killing or a wildly inaccurate doctor.

Anton can become cooperative before Vera does, but he must not serve as the game's final password. He may admit movement while refusing to accuse his sister. He may describe what he saw while continuing to call it an accident. The player can use partial truth and physical evidence to reach the answer.

## 16. Neutral lives and independent story threads

The hotel does not divide neatly into suspects and clue dispensers. Rosa and Pavel are negotiating where they will live. Jakob and Emily are trying to keep transplanted plants alive. Benno wants the reception food delivered before it is wasted. Saskia is deciding whether her reopening photographs should become news photographs. These concerns can create conversations, favors, and observations without becoming compulsory chores.

Oskar Meier, the funicular operator, is helping a late visitor reach the residence. Juri Weiss, the mechanic, wants a faulty door properly repaired before a public tour. Marta is protecting guests' privacy while trying to reunite coats with their owners. David wants the investigator to distinguish an archived agreement from the popular story everyone repeats about it.

Celia, the exhibition volunteer, has used an undated photograph under a confident caption. Correcting it leads to an affectionate argument about how the sanatorium is remembered, not a buried serial crime. A resident's right to occupy a room rests on the actual agreement, not on winning this historical disagreement.

A valuable plant has been moved without being logged because Jakob is protecting it from a cold delivery bay. He admits this once he understands the route matters. Its movement explains some soil and trolley activity while leaving the homicide route independently demonstrable. The player learns that a suspicious trace can have more than one source.

These threads should create a sense of people continuing to live around the investigation. They offer optional emotional closure and practical assistance. Essential evidence never requires winning a plant-care mini-game, performing unpaid errands, or becoming friends with every NPC.

## 17. What changes during play

The night gives way to a clear, cold morning. Breakfast changes where people gather. The baths close for cleaning and reopen in daylight. The residence common room fills with people comparing notices. The same space can feel different without requiring a second asset set for an entire town.

Confirmed disclosure of the annex terms changes residents' behavior. They organize a meeting and ask direct questions of Vera and Richard. The player can attend, observe, or continue elsewhere. Their concerns are not paused until the investigator clicks the next clue.

If Hannes learns that the hamper has been traced, he tries to leave for an already scheduled supplier trip. The departure is signaled through an ordinary conversation and visible packing. Missing it changes his interview to a telephone or later official interview; it does not erase the recovered weight or make the case fail.

If Irene learns the duplicate approval has been found, she seeks a private conversation and may offer the original carbon pad in exchange for being heard. The investigator may accept the meeting, involve Lena, or rely on independently recovered documents. The game does not demand a false promise of immunity.

If Vera becomes aware of the textile comparison, she changes her explanation from no private meeting to an accidental struggle. Previously recorded words remain on the evidence board. The model must not rewrite her history to make the new account retrospectively consistent.

There is no additional murder inserted solely to increase urgency. Threats, departure, and attempted manipulation have proportionate consequences and visible warning. The lone investigator is never stranded in a two-character rescue mechanic and cannot lose the entire case through an unannounced deadline.

## 18. Ending and proof standards

The player presents a reconstruction in their own language. It should answer where Felix was assaulted, why the fall scene is misleading, why Vera is identified as the killer, what the others knowingly did, and why Felix's disclosure mattered. Exact minute values and the game's internal clue IDs are not required.

A strong accusation distinguishes Irene's forgery and coercion, Hannes's confinement and later disposal, and Anton's post-death assistance. An accusation that correctly identifies Vera but carelessly assigns the blows to everyone receives a specific unresolved question, not an arbitrary failure or a full spoiler explanation.

An adequately supported early solution is accepted. There is no rule preserving a minimum number of suspects through hour ten. The twenty-hour target comes from the breadth and quality of available investigation, not a prohibition on being clever.

Vera's final response depends on the evidence shown and her remaining room to maneuver. She can concede the meeting, blame Felix, plead accident, or fall silent. She does not become a villain who explains every hidden detail because the player has reached a cutscene. The reconstruction can stand against her denial.

The immediate resolution secures the evidence and prevents the false accident account from becoming accepted. Separate epilogue scenes address the residence agreement, the staff cooperative, the reopening, and the victim's relationships. Solving a murder does not automatically repair the hotel's finances.

The player may help the residents obtain an accurate public account, preserve relevant records, and hear Clara decide what should happen to Felix's personal folder. Optional completion changes these human outcomes without changing the fixed murderer or rewarding a morally prescribed dialogue style.

## 19. What must remain private during production

Floor-plan reviews use room functions and neutral furniture labels. The potting room remains an ordinary potting room in review; it is not labeled as the true scene. Substitute discovery objects exercise the same interaction types in unrelated positions. The user may learn the geography, but not the route of the body or which physical details are decisive.

NPC image and video prompts contain only public appearance, occupation, habitual movement, and ordinary setting. Do not include guilt, concealed fear, defensive injuries, secret relationships, accomplice categories, or instructions to look sinister. Vera and the neutral staff receive the same visual care.

Real evidence illustrations, undiscovered testimony, hidden connections, and the solution stay out of public browser payloads. A separate review fixture uses neutral labels and an independent save. This is a future implementation requirement, not a claim that the current server has already implemented protected asset delivery.

The next action after saving this expanded package is to report readiness for the separate critique. Do not write that critique or start investigator artwork until the user advances the checkpoint.

## 20. Four authored scenes that give the investigation human weight

### Supper after the reception

The hotel has prepared more food than anyone now feels able to eat. Benno sets a staff table in the service dining room and asks people to take something before it goes cold. This scene can happen while the investigator follows another lead; it is not a mandatory cutscene.

Vera notices that Emily has not eaten and puts a plate within her reach. The gesture is sincere. Anton begins a familiar joke and loses the ending.
