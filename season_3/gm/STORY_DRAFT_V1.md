# The Winter Garden — original private story draft

SPOILERS. Historical draft, retained for the critique pass. Once GROUND_TRUTH.md exists, it is the authority; never mix draft facts into play. Do not expose this document or describe its contents to the player.

## Premise and people

Bad Auersee is a fictional German-speaking Alpine spa settlement. Its former Sonnenfels sanatorium is reopening as a thermal hotel on Friday, 27 November 1998. The opening has brought hotel staff, former sanatorium employees, craftspeople, guests, and a regional lender into the valley. The investigator arrives alone after a death inside the glass winter garden. Local officer Lena Ortner has kept the winter garden closed and called for outside help. Weather complicates travel but does not create an implausibly absolute blockade.

Felix Arendt, 46, the restoration architect, lies beside an overturned planter below the winter garden's upper gallery. Staff initially suspect a fall through a temporarily dismantled railing. He was admired for preserving shared spaces and disliked for delaying opening decisions. He was neither secretly a criminal mastermind nor universally loved.

Vera Stein, 44, the hotel's operations director, killed Felix. She has spent years keeping the restoration alive and believes she alone has held the community together. Without telling the staff cooperative, she pledged the east residential annex as collateral under a financing agreement, contrary to the cooperative's approval. She used a copied signature from Felix to represent the annex conversion as agreed and technically approved. The new financing allows staff flats and the publicly used winter room to become private suites if the hotel misses a payment. Felix found the substituted agreement and intended to give the cooperative the original correspondence that evening. Vera feared personal exposure as well as losing the hotel.

During a private meeting in the potting room, Vera struck Felix with a detachable iron conservatory vent crank. Her brother Anton Stein, 50, the porter, subsequently helped her move the body under the gallery. Vera told him Felix had fallen against the bench in an argument. Anton believed this at first but understood that moving a body and staging a fall was concealment. He is an accessory after the death, not a planner or second killer. No other NPC participated in the killing.

## Draft chronology

- 17:40: Felix receives confirmation that the annex agreement bearing his signature was submitted to the lender.
- 18:05: Felix and lender representative Richard Kessler argue publicly. Richard demands a viable reopening plan. He did not know Felix's approval had been copied.
- 18:20: Felix speaks with resident Rosa Leitner about the benches being removed from the east winter room. He promises to attend the staff meeting.
- 18:35: Vera invites Felix to the potting room, ostensibly to review planting and reception arrangements.
- 18:45: Felix refuses to delay disclosure. Vera strikes him from behind as he bends over the plan chest. He dies there. The blow is intentional, although she has not arrived with a prepared murder plan.
- 18:50: Vera summons Anton via the internal telephone. She describes an accident and asks him to keep the guests away.
- 18:55–19:05: They move Felix on a plant trolley and lay him below the upper gallery. Anton dislodges a planter; Vera removes a railing section already loosened for restoration work. They leave a supposed fall scene.
- 19:12: Apprentice gardener Emily Novak enters and finds Felix. She raises the alarm.
- 20:00: The investigator arrives. The first accessible location is the winter garden.

## Investigation shape

The opening establishes uncertainty about an accident, the victim's appointment, and obvious routes to speak with staff. There should be no need to guess that the potting room is searchable. The winter garden includes ground-level planting, a gallery, a potting room, a small service vestibule, and a public entrance. Search leads to trolley marks, disturbed soil, and a cleaned workbench.

Early suspects are Vera, Richard, gardener Jakob Reiter, and singer Clara Weiss. Jakob had argued with Felix about removal of mature plants. Clara had ended a relationship with Felix and wants to recover personal letters before staff gossip spreads. Both have ordinary explanations rather than unrelated major crimes. Richard is aggressive about the hotel's finances, but difficult manners do not prove murder.

The player can inspect the proposed fall, follow the plant trolley, question staff, or examine the disputed building plans. These branches meet around the potting room and annex agreement. The most damning draft evidence is a recorded answering-machine message from Felix saying he is meeting Vera about the forged signature. Vera's cuff bears blood, and Anton eventually admits moving the body.

A final reconstruction identifies the potting room as the scene, the crank as the likely weapon, Vera as the killer, Anton as the mover, and the forged annex approval as motive. The draft ending asks the player to present a fixed set of evidence cards, after which Anton confirms the reconstruction and Vera responds.

## Cast intentions

- Vera: capable, hospitable, remembers everybody's practical needs. Cooperation is partly real and partly an attempt to follow the investigation.
- Anton: sociable porter, proud of his sister, ashamed of his decision; he can give useful truthful directions while concealing what he did.
- Lena: helpful local officer, dry humor, no personal conspiracy; respects independent reasoning.
- Richard: lender representative, brisk and socially clumsy; fears a failed project and his own lax checking of the approval.
- Rosa: retired sanatorium cook and annex resident; funny, sociable, angry about being spoken for, not a helpless source of pathos.
- Jakob: head gardener, exuberant about plants, disappointed by Felix but able to distinguish disappointment from hatred.
- Emily: apprentice, eager to help, shaken by discovering the body; unsure of times, confident about what plants were moved.
- Clara: returning singer booked for reopening; dislikes being reduced to Felix's former partner and has genuine interest in the restoration.
- Dr. Miriam Fuchs: local physician, straightforward about limits, arranges independent examination instead of providing magical precision.
- Nora Baum: carpenter and restoration foreperson; direct, generous with explanations, admits leaving a railing temporarily unsecured.
- Oskar Meier: funicular operator, likes company, a reliable source about movements but not an omniscient clock.

## Locations proposed

Winter Garden; Grand Hotel public and staff rooms; Thermal Baths; East Residence; Restoration Workshop; Funicular Station and platform; Village Café and square; Municipal Reading Room. Each should contain life and actions beyond clue collection. The reading room hosts later expert reports instead of requiring a whole laboratory scene.

## Development questions deliberately left for critique

Is the financial conflict too abstract? Does the cuff plus message identify Vera too mechanically? Is Anton a confession gate? Does staged falling require too much unsupported forensic certainty? Do multiple scene discoveries merely deliver the same deduction? Can a direct early search solve the case fairly? Can reviewing the potting room spoil the murder? How can the victim be a person instead of a moral prop? What changes when the player is rude, distrusts the police, or accuses someone early?
