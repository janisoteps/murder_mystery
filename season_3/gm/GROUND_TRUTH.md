# Season 3 ground truth — *The Winter Garden*

> GAME-MASTER EYES ONLY. This is the authoritative story. Never quote, summarize, link, or expose any concealed fact to the player. Earlier drafts and the private critique are development history; where they differ, this file governs.

## Canon status and design promise

The season is a roughly twenty-hour first-play investigation for one player-controlled investigator. Twenty hours is a content and pacing target, not a gate. A player who forms and supports the correct reconstruction early must be allowed to proceed.

The mystery has one murderer. Three other people knowingly commit related wrongdoing at different times and with different information. Their acts must never collapse into a generic four-person conspiracy. Several innocent people lie, misremember, hide private conduct, or make serious mistakes. Those acts remain consequential without turning them into murder accomplices.

The solution is fixed before implementation. No model may choose a culprit, manufacture an alibi, move an undiscovered object, or reinterpret a neutral NPC as guilty during play.

## Setting

Bad Auersee is a fictional German-speaking Alpine spa settlement. The game begins on Friday, 27 November 1998, during the reopening weekend of Sonnenfels, a former sanatorium converted into a thermal hotel.

The complex stands above the village. The original glass winter garden connects the main hotel, an occupied east residence, and the thermal baths through public paths and service passages. A funicular links the upper terrace to the village square. The weather brings snow, cold air, and shifting visibility, but roads and official communication remain available. Isolation is atmospheric rather than an implausible reason nobody else can help.

The reopening includes a reception, a modest concert, public tours, and a historical exhibition. Staff have accepted deferred wages and difficult conditions to complete the restoration. Fourteen people still live in east-wing flats. Their shared winter room, garden access, and therapy sessions are protected by the project's approved occupancy agreement.

## Victim

Felix Arendt, 46, is the restoration architect and an independent signatory required for the final construction draw. He fought successfully to preserve the winter garden, but he also approved a temporary construction route that made the east residents carry shopping and laundry along a steep service corridor. He dismissed their first objections as temporary inconvenience. Only after Rosa Leitner made him walk the route carrying her bags did he revise the access plan.

Felix is perceptive about spaces and often poor at sharing control. He sketches while others speak, sometimes making them feel observed rather than heard. He and singer Clara Weiss ended a serious relationship after Felix used private experiences in a public design lecture without asking her. His later apology is sincere but incomplete. Clara has every right to remain angry.

Felix recently learned that a financing packet included a conversion schedule he explicitly rejected. The schedule bears a copied version of his signature and represents the residents and staff cooperative as consenting. At 19:30 on the night of his death, he intends to place the approved occupancy agreement, his written rejection, and a corrective statement before the residents, lender representative, and staff delegates. He will suspend his completion certification until the false schedule is withdrawn.

His action is ethically right and financially dangerous. Without the final loan draw, current wages and supplier debts cannot all be paid. Felix understands that risk but refuses to make occupied homes the unapproved security for it. He also intends to admit publicly that his own earlier access decision helped management normalize treating the residence as movable space.

## The concealed financing change

Six weeks before opening, operations director Vera Stein learned that restoration overruns had exhausted the bridge facility. The lender would release the final draw only if more revenue-producing space entered the security package.

Vera submitted **Schedule East-14**, which converts fourteen occupied flats and the residents' shared winter room into hotel suites if the first post-opening payment is missed. The schedule also permits immediate measurement, lock preparation, and furnishing work. The staff cooperative approved a loan against hotel operating revenue; it did not approve Schedule East-14 or pledge the residence.

The scheme has one legible consequence: people can lose the rooms and shared space they currently inhabit. The financial documents exist to prove consent and responsibility, not to require accounting expertise.

Irene Seidel, the cooperative bookkeeper, assembled the false approval page. She copied Felix's signature from an earlier circulation drawing and attached it to Schedule East-14. She also represented an informal staff discussion as a cooperative vote. She knew both acts were false. She believed Vera would obtain real signatures once opening revenue arrived.

Hannes Kroll, the restoration contractor, accepted advance payment to measure doors, prepare lock cylinders, and mark future suite partitions in the east residence. He told his crew the work was temporary storage preparation. His company was close to failing and several employees had personally advanced money for materials. He knew the residents had not agreed.

Richard Kessler, the lender representative, accepted the submitted packet without checking consent directly. He did not forge it and was not told that Felix had rejected the schedule. He suspected the residents did not understand the conversion clause and privately visited them before the death instead of reporting that concern to his office. His negligence and self-protection are real; he is innocent of coercion and murder.

## The coercion plan

At 18:08, Vera, Irene, and Hannes meet in the hotel's service office. Vera has learned that Felix possesses his original rejection letter and plans to speak at 19:30. She proposes a coercive plan:

- Irene will take the annex papers from Felix's briefcase and return the remaining contents.
- Hannes will use a maintenance screen and service key to keep Felix inside the winter-garden delivery vestibule.
- Vera will force Felix to sign a short withdrawal and agree that the meeting is postponed.
- If he refuses, they will keep him away from the residents until after the lender's final transmission deadline that evening.

All three understand that Felix is not attending voluntarily and that Hannes will prevent him leaving. None arrives intending murder. That fact limits their initial plan; it does not make it innocent.

Irene has already made a private carbon copy of Felix's rejection letter. She keeps it as protection against Vera, though she tells herself it is an accounting safeguard. This decision later undermines the cover story.

## Distinct responsibility

### Vera Stein

Vera created Schedule East-14, directed the coercion, remained alone with Felix after the others left, and deliberately killed him. She then directed the false-fall story and manipulated each helper with a different partial explanation.

Vera is not a secret sadist or career criminal. She has performed years of indispensable work and genuinely cares for staff and residents. Her central flaw is believing that being the person who keeps everything alive gives her the right to decide what everyone else must risk.

### Irene Seidel

Irene forged the approval, misrepresented the staff vote, removed papers from Felix's bag during the coercion, and later altered the posted meeting record to hide her own document crimes. She left before the fatal assault and did not move the body or weapon.

After the death, Vera tells Irene that Hannes struck Felix during the restraint and that the fall scene protects the whole staff from his negligence. Irene suspects this is incomplete but does not know Vera delivered the fatal blows. Her retained carbon copy contradicts Vera's claim that Felix had reconsidered.

### Hannes Kroll

Hannes concealed the residence work, blocked Felix's exit, physically restrained him, and caused a split lip and wrist injury during the struggle. He left Felix alive. After the killing, Vera tells him Felix fell against the botanical press after their argument and says Hannes's earlier violence will make him appear responsible. Hannes removes a component from the press area, routes it through contractor storage, falsifies his work time, and attempts to shift blame toward Nora's unsecured railing.

Hannes understands by then that he is concealing a death. He does not know Vera struck Felix twice, and he believes the object he removes caused an accidental fatal injury. His physical contact, financial motive, false record, access control, and disposal act make him the strongest wrong solution.

### Anton Stein

Anton, Vera's older brother and the head porter, has no knowledge of the financing forgery or coercion meeting beforehand. Vera calls him after the murder and says Felix fell during a fight with Hannes. Anton sees that Felix was injured in the potting room and helps move him to the palm hall. He participates in staging the fall.

Anton returns alone several minutes later to straighten Felix's collar and cover his upper chest. During that return he notices a second impact mark inconsistent with the single accident Vera described. He takes Vera's torn cardigan from a laundry basket after she asks him to dispose of it, but hides it in a locked porter trunk instead. From that point he knowingly protects Vera while preserving the item because he cannot quite destroy it.

## Innocent but consequential conduct

Nora Baum left the gallery cordon less secure than required and initially overstates it. The safety failure is real, though the gallery fall is staged.

Jakob Reiter moved a valuable plant without completing its inventory record to protect it from a cold delivery bay. This creates an innocent trolley journey and soil deposits that complicate route evidence.

Clara Weiss takes a personal blue folder from the cloakroom with Felix's permission. Their dispute concerns ownership of private letters and sketches. It is unrelated to the annex papers.

Richard visits the residence without telling his employer and minimizes his doubts about consent. He argues publicly with Felix about the disputed signature.

Amalia Roth sincerely associates seeing Felix at the thermal baths with her 18:52 payment receipt. She actually greeted him before changing, waiting for gloves, and paying. She is mistaken, not lying.

Photographer Saskia Dorn considers selling reopening photographs after the death but ultimately lets Lena preserve relevant negatives first. Her ambition is not evidence of involvement.

## Fixed physical geography

The Winter Garden destination contains six connected areas: public entrance court, central palm hall, upper gallery, potting room, delivery vestibule, and a sheltered exterior service path.

The potting room and palm hall are on the same level. A plant trolley can pass through the delivery vestibule without stairs. The upper gallery overlooks the palm hall but not the potting room. A railing section has been removed for restoration and marked by a movable cordon.

The delivery vestibule has a wall telephone, a staff desk, and two exits: the palm hall and sheltered service path. Hannes positions a folding work screen across the public sightline and holds the service-path door. There is no secret passage.

The potting room contains a plan chest, workbench, washing sink, botanical specimen press, and ventilation equipment. The press uses two interchangeable cast-iron compression plates seated on felt pads. Vera uses one plate as the weapon. Afterward, she returns the plate to the press with its wiped face downward. She asks Hannes to remove “the iron from the press.” Hannes removes the wrong loose component: a smaller removable winding handle stained by transfer from Vera's wiping cloth. He never sees the plate's underside.

This error is central. The object Hannes hides looks incriminating and carries victim blood, but the injury shape and missing felt impression do not match it. The true weapon remains in the ordinary press until a later comparison justifies dismantling it. The game does not hide the final weapon in a magical locked container or place it visibly beside the body.

## Exact chronology

Times are authoring truth. Player-facing sources have different precision. Accept correct intervals in the final reconstruction.

### Afternoon preparations

**15:10–15:28.** Richard receives the full submitted loan packet at the hotel guest office. Irene sees the incoming fax and alerts Vera that Felix requested the annex attachment. Saskia takes preparation photographs in the winter garden. Her sequence shows both iron press plates seated, the winding handle on its hook, the work screen folded beside the vestibule, and Nora's cordon in its original position.

**16:20.** Felix receives a copy of Schedule East-14. He recognizes that his signature has been copied from an earlier circulation drawing. He telephones municipal records clerk David Suter, who agrees to place the approved occupancy agreement and Felix's registered rejection in the reading-room return locker.

**16:45–17:00.** Felix visits the east residence. He tells Rosa and Pavel that he will explain the schedule at 19:30 and acknowledge his earlier access error. Pavel keeps a measurement sketch showing suite marks already made in his room.

**17:05–17:18.** Felix and Nora inspect the unfinished gallery rail in the workshop. He initials her drawing and knows the cordon must remain. Hannes hears part of the discussion.

**17:25.** Felix gives Clara permission to collect the personal blue folder from the hotel cloakroom. Marta Huber sees the exchange. The folder contains letters and sketches, not financing records.

**17:50–18:00.** Felix and Richard argue in the hotel corridor. Benno hears “signature,” “fourteen rooms,” and anger, but no threat. Richard leaves by the east-residence path to ask the residents what they were told.

### Coercion and first violence

**18:08–18:15.** Vera, Irene, and Hannes agree on the coercion plan in the service office. Irene writes a short routing note on the back of an invoice stub. She later tears it in half; one part remains under the blotter.

**18:18.** Hannes sends gardening apprentice Emil Novak to deliver a cold-sensitive plant to the lower funicular goods room. The errand is real and harmless; its timing clears the vestibule. Hannes unfolds the work screen and moves the maintenance notice from the gallery stair to the service approach.

**18:24.** Felix stops briefly at the baths for a borrowed umbrella. Amalia greets him before going to change. Marta remembers the umbrella and the order of Amalia's later checkout.

**18:31.** Felix enters the delivery vestibule for Vera's requested meeting. Irene takes his briefcase to the adjacent service desk under the pretense of finding the relevant page. Hotel runner Tess Moser hears Felix demand his bag back through the screen.

**18:34–18:36.** Irene removes Felix's original rejection and approved-plan copy. She leaves ordinary site papers and the personal contents. Her sleeve catches the torn routing note beneath the blotter. She returns to the hotel office with the papers.

**18:36–18:39.** Felix attempts to leave through the service path. Hannes blocks him. During the struggle Hannes grips Felix's wrist and pushes him against the vestibule desk, splitting Felix's lip. Blood transfers to Hannes's canvas apron and falls beneath the brass floor grate. Tess hears the impact and Felix say, “Take your hands off me.” She cannot see the speakers clearly.

**18:39–18:41.** Vera orders Hannes away, saying his presence is making agreement impossible. Hannes passes her the service key. Nora and Juri see Hannes enter the workshop at 18:43 carrying a bent hinge bracket. They work with him continuously until Vera summons him later. Irene is seen back in the accounts office by Benno at 18:42 when he delivers voucher slips.

### Murder

**18:41–18:43.** Vera leads Felix into the potting room, saying she will return the rejected schedule if he signs a postponement. Felix refuses. He says he will put both versions before the residents at 19:30 and suspend certification before the lender deadline.

**18:43–18:46.** Felix turns toward the plan chest to recover his papers. Vera lifts one iron compression plate from the open botanical press and strikes the back-left side of his head. Felix falls against the plan chest, catches the left sleeve of Vera's dark green cardigan, and pulls free a blood-wicked wool fragment as he tries to rise. The fragment drops beneath the chest lip. Vera strikes a second time. The two injuries are distinct from the earlier split lip and wrist bruising.

At **18:44**, the winter garden's automatic misting line activates for ninety seconds. The potting-room supply pipe records a brief pressure dip because the sink tap is running while Vera washes the plate. Mist settles over the outer part of the trolley route later, while the earliest blood deposits beneath the plan chest remain dry and partly protected. This supports sequence and an interval; it does not identify Vera by itself.

Vera wipes the plate's visible face and returns it to the press upside down. Blood remains beneath the felt seat and within a casting flaw. She places the wiping cloth in a laundry basket and calls the porter desk from the vestibule telephone at 18:47.

### Movement and false scene

**18:50.** Anton arrives. Vera says Hannes knocked Felix into the press and ran. Anton sees the blood by the plan chest and urges her to call Miriam. Vera says the scandal will destroy the hotel and Hannes will be blamed regardless. Anton decides to help.

**18:52–18:57.** Vera and Anton place Felix on a plant trolley. A damaged wheel leaves interrupted grit marks from the potting room through the vestibule to the palm hall. Some outer tracks pass over fresh mist; inner protected deposits predate it. They place Felix below the gallery opening, dislodge a low planter, move the cordon, and arrange one shoe as if it came loose in a fall.

**18:57.** Vera calls Hannes from the gallery service extension. Juri hears the workshop phone and sees Hannes leave. Vera tells Hannes that Felix fell against the press after their struggle and that the blood on Hannes's apron will make the death his fault. She instructs him to remove the iron component and align the maintenance record with an earlier accident. Before returning to reception, she puts on a spare hotel blazer from the service coat hook, leaves the torn cardigan in the laundry basket, and tells Anton to burn it once the scene is discovered.

**18:59–19:03.** Hannes wraps and removes the press winding handle, whose end received victim blood from the wiping cloth in the basket. He does not inspect the compression plates. He places the handle in a contractor hamper routed to the thermal service room and backdates his job entry. He also tells Nora that her unsafe cordon may have killed Felix.

**19:02.** Vera returns to the hotel reception and asks Benno for a fresh drink. Guests remember her at different moments but cannot place her continuously before this time.

**19:04–19:06.** Anton returns alone to the palm hall. He straightens Felix's collar and covers the upper chest with Felix's coat. From the gallery stair, Celia Maurer sees a porter-uniformed figure bending over the body. Anton notices the second head impact and realizes Vera's single-fall account is false.

**19:07.** Anton returns to the service laundry basket for Vera's torn cardigan. Instead of carrying out her earlier instruction, he locks it in his private porter trunk.

**19:10–19:12.** Emil returns for the plant trolley and discovers Felix. He shouts. Jakob and Dr. Miriam Fuchs arrive, followed by Vera and others. Miriam checks Felix and asks that nobody move him. Jakob shifts one pot so she can kneel and reports doing so.

### First concealment after discovery

**19:14–19:20.** Irene hears of the death. Vera tells her Hannes caused it and the false fall protects the hotel and cooperative. Irene replaces a posted meeting note with one saying Felix agreed to postpone. She does not destroy her private carbon of his rejection.

**19:20–19:30.** Hannes completes the false job entry and prepares a normal supplier trip scheduled for the next morning. Nora notes privately that his claim about the cordon does not match their 17:05 discussion.

**19:30 onward.** Local officer Lena Ortner secures the winter garden, separates initial statements, preserves photographs, and requests outside investigative help and regional medical examination. She does not accept the fall as established.

**20:05.** The investigator arrives alone. Only the Winter Garden is initially accessible on the map.

## What each principal participant believes at 20:05

Vera knows the complete current crime and the financing scheme. She believes Hannes removed the iron plate, not the winding handle. She does not know Irene retained a carbon copy or Anton kept the cardigan.

Irene knows the financing fraud, coercion plan, paper removal, and her own altered note. She believes Hannes's restraint caused Felix's death and Vera staged the scene to protect the project. She suspects Vera is omitting something because the timing is poor, but has no direct murder knowledge.

Hannes knows the financing fraud, coercion, his own nonfatal violence, Vera's later claim of an accidental fall, removal of the bloody handle, and the false job entry. He believes Vera and Anton staged the body. He does not know the iron plate is the true weapon.

Anton knows the body was moved from the potting room, Vera directed the staging, the body has two head impacts, and Vera's torn garment exists. He believes Vera killed Felix but has not seen the act. He does not know the financing details or Irene and Hannes's planned coercion.

This asymmetry governs dialogue. No model may flatten their knowledge into a shared conspiracy history.

## Principal physical proof

The case does not depend on confession. A defensible reconstruction can be built from:

- injury differentiation: the vestibule restraint injuries are nonfatal and distinct from two later head impacts;
- site sequence: dry protected blood at the plan chest, mist-affected outer trolley route, then staging in the palm hall;
- workshop interval: overlapping human activity places Hannes away for the fatal assault but returns him for the later disposal;
- the wrong removed object: Hannes hides a bloody winding handle whose geometry does not match the fatal impacts;
- the true weapon: an inverted compression plate retains victim blood beneath its felt seat and damage consistent with the impacts;
- defensive contact: a blood-wicked dark-green wool fragment from the potting room matches the torn edge of Vera's cardigan, not Hannes's canvas apron;
- opportunity and control: Vera remains alone with Felix, makes the porter call, directs later actions, and changes her account as each layer becomes public;
- motive: Schedule East-14, false consent, and Felix's imminent certification withdrawal establish what she stood to lose.

No one object says “Vera did it.” The murder case needs place, sequence, opportunity, contact, and motive. Motive without assault proof is insufficient; textile without its blood-and-location context is insufficient.

## Strong wrong solution

The intended midpoint theory identifies Hannes as the killer and Vera as the manager who staged a workplace death to save the hotel.

It is supported by real facts: Hannes concealed residence work, arranged the screen and errand, physically restrained Felix, had the victim's blood on his apron, controlled the exit, possessed the service key, removed a bloodied iron component, falsified his time, blamed Nora, and planned to leave in the morning.

It fails for earned reasons:

- the blood on Hannes's apron and beneath the vestibule grate belongs to the earlier split-lip struggle;
- the fatal injuries occurred later in the potting room;
- Hannes is independently in the workshop during that later interval;
- the removed handle is not the fatal object;
- Vera remains with Felix and carries the blood-wicked defensive textile match;
- her 18:47 call precedes the movement and later summons Hannes for disposal.

The game must never call the player foolish for accepting the Hannes theory. It is a rational synthesis of incomplete evidence. Its collapse should feel like better classification, not a twist that invalidates earlier observations.

## Secondary suspicions

Richard has motive to suppress Felix, a public argument, access to the false packet, and an initially concealed residence visit. His overlapping interactions at the residence exclude the fatal act while exposing his professional negligence.

Anton is observed over the body before the alarm, lies about when he first approached, has route traces on his porter clothing, and hides Vera's cardigan. These prove serious after-the-fact conduct. His ordinary luggage tasks and the timing of the call place him away during the murder.

Clara takes an apparently important folder after arguing with Felix about private material. The contents, permission, cloakroom sequence, and preparation photographs show it is a separate personal dispute. Resolving it deepens the victim without functioning as a complete murder theory.

Nora's unsecured cordon and fear support negligence concealment. Felix's knowledge of the work, the staged route, and assault evidence show that a real safety failure was exploited rather than causing the death.

## Required final reconstruction

The investigator should establish, in ordinary language:

1. Felix was fatally assaulted in the potting room and moved beneath the gallery.
2. Hannes restrained Felix earlier and later concealed an object, but did not deliver the fatal blows.
3. Vera remained alone with Felix, struck him twice with the press plate, summoned Anton, and directed the staged fall.
4. Irene knowingly forged consent and removed papers during the coercion; she later altered the meeting record without witnessing the killing.
5. Anton knowingly moved and rearranged the body and preserved the cardigan after realizing Vera's account was false.
6. Schedule East-14 and Felix's intended withdrawal explain the coercion and Vera's escalation.

Exact minutes, internal clue IDs, legal terminology, and completion of unrelated personal threads are never required.

## Final dramatic situation

Once the investigator requests a formal reconstruction and sufficient support exists, Lena arranges it in the east residence's shared winter room before the scheduled plan meeting. The disputed space is visible around the participants. This is not automatically a public accusation: the investigator can ask Lena to limit attendance to the necessary people or allow resident representatives already present to remain.

Vera arrives intending to preserve the conversion agreement and blame Hannes. Hannes arrives intending to expose Vera's instruction while minimizing his coercion. Irene brings or can be confronted with the carbon copy. Anton must decide whether to continue protecting Vera after the second-injury sequence is stated. Their conflicting interests create the drama; a single theatrical confession does not solve the case.

The investigator chooses which account to test first and may present the reconstruction in their own order. Lena asks only focused questions about unsupported links. Prior interviews remain authoritative; characters cannot revise history without the contradiction being visible.

Vera may concede the meeting, blame Hannes, call the death an accident, or refuse further speech. The physical reconstruction stands without confession. A correct conclusion leads to evidence preservation and official action. The story does not assign the player an emotion or political judgment.

## Aftermath

The murder case and the future of Sonnenfels are separate outcomes. Vera's removal does not pay wages or automatically save the residence. The false schedule is suspended because its consent record is exposed, while the staff and residents face a real decision about financing and reopening.

Richard must report the lender's failure to verify the schedule. Irene and Hannes face consequences for their own actions. Anton's cooperation, timing, and preservation of evidence affect how his conduct is understood but do not erase it.

Clara decides what happens to Felix's personal material. Nora corrects the safety record. Jakob and Emil restore ordinary life to the winter garden after evidence is released. Rosa and Pavel retain agency in the residence negotiations.

Optional human outcomes depend on what the investigator disclosed, promises actually made, and how private material was handled. They never change the culprit or replace the proof standard.

## Non-negotiable fairness rules

- The true weapon, garment, copied schedule, and relevant reports cannot be destroyed by an unseen timer.
- Essential propositions have at least two routes or a preserved source after a live interaction is missed.
- The investigator may inspect the potting room during the opening; early access does not reveal the entire role chain.
- No confession is mandatory.
- No exact dialogue phrase is mandatory.
- No friendship score unlocks core evidence.
- No receipt, ticket, machine log, or photograph is treated as proving more than it records.
- An unsupported correct guess receives a request for grounds, never confirmation through UI, animation, or narrator voice.
- A supported early reconstruction is accepted.
- Innocent characters do not become hostile merely to preserve difficulty.
- The player's speech, thoughts, decisions, identity, and emotional reaction remain player-controlled.
