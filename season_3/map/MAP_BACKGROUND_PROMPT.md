# Sonnenfels map background prompt

This is a spoiler-free production brief for the painted Season 3 map background. The generated image must contain no text, labels, pins, locks, interface elements, evidence, dramatic figures, or story-specific events. Interactive location names and discovery states will be layered above it in the game.

## Generation prompt

Create an exceptionally detailed, hand-painted bird's-eye investigation map of **Sonnenfels**, a fictional German-speaking Alpine spa settlement in late November 1998. Wide cinematic 16:9 composition, consistent elevated three-quarter/isometric-like projection, approximately 35–40 degrees above the landscape. The visual language should match a premium illustrated murder-mystery map: believable architecture and geography, fine ink-like architectural edges beneath rich digital gouache painting, restrained realism, intricate environmental storytelling, elegant rather than whimsical.

The terrain rises steeply from a compact old village in the lower foreground to a broad mountain terrace in the upper middle. Snow has recently fallen: thin uneven snow on roofs, lawns, stone walls, fir trees, and mountain paths, with patches of dark wet earth and cleared paving still visible. It is blue hour just after sunset. The snow and sky provide luminous cool blue-gray ambient light, while selected windows and exterior lamps glow muted amber. The scene should remain clear and readable rather than dark, foggy, monochrome, or horror-stylized. Distant forested mountains frame the top edge without hiding the settlement.

The **upper mountain terrace** is the main visual focus and occupies roughly the middle 55% of the frame. Build a coherent historic sanatorium complex converted into a thermal hotel, with each destination visually distinct and separated by readable courtyards, paths, terraces, or gardens:

- A grand late-19th-century Alpine hotel of pale stone and dark slate, four stories, symmetrical but accumulated through several building phases, with a formal entrance court and warmly lit public windows.
- At the heart of the complex, a magnificent long **glass Winter Garden** conservatory connecting the hotel buildings: iron ribs, tall glazed walls, a central glass roof, visible palms and dense botanical silhouettes, condensation, and several softly glowing interior lamps. Give it a clear forecourt because this is the initial map destination.
- A substantial historic **thermal-baths wing** on a lower terrace, recognizable through arched windows, masonry bath halls, discreet steam rising from two outdoor pools, and covered connections to the main complex.
- An inhabited **east residence**, clearly more domestic than the hotel: a long masonry residential wing with balconies, varied curtains, laundry details, and many individual windows.
- A practical **restoration workshop** behind and to the left of the main complex: sawtooth or pitched workshop roofs, a loading court, stacked timber and stone, covered work areas, and service access.
- A small detached **architect's lodging**, an elegant but modest period chalet or converted gate lodge near a quiet side road, visibly separate from the hotel.
- A **gardener's cottage and nursery** beside terraced planting beds: small cottage, potting sheds, cold frames, service greenhouse, dormant orchard rows, stacked pots, and a winding garden path.
- A graceful **panorama pavilion** on a separate overlook at the upper-right edge: an octagonal or apsidal Belle Époque hall with tall windows and a broad mountain-view terrace, reached by a curving path.

A clearly visible **funicular railway** descends diagonally from the upper resort terrace toward the village. Show twin rails, one small period carriage, pylons or cable infrastructure, and architecturally distinct upper and lower station buildings. The route must read instantly and should visually connect the two halves of the map without cutting through other landmarks.

The **lower village** occupies roughly the bottom 30% of the frame. It is compact and lived-in, arranged around a small paved square with a fountain or bare winter tree, surrounded by restrained 18th- and 19th-century Alpine buildings. Include three distinct destinations with clear roofs and small open spaces around them:

- A welcoming **village café** on the square, with awning, terrace furniture stacked for winter, and warm ground-floor windows.
- A dignified small **municipal reading room**, identifiable as a civic library/archive building with tall windows, stone steps, and a modest clock or notice case—but no readable writing.
- A modest combined **police and medical building**, sober and practical, with a covered vehicle bay and a small lamp-lit entrance—but no symbols, crosses, lettering, or signage.

Connect the entire settlement with believable roads, stone stairs, covered walks, retaining walls, and service paths. Add ordinary background life through parked late-1990s European cars, a delivery van, stacked firewood, cleared snow, chimney smoke, footprints, garden tools, benches, utility lamps, and a few tiny distant silhouettes. Do not place prominent people or depict any crime.

Composition requirements: every named destination must have a unique silhouette and at least one uncluttered patch of ground or roof edge nearby for a future interactive marker and label. Keep important buildings away from the outer 5% of the canvas. Avoid overlapping landmarks, deep black shadows, heavy snowfall, thick fog, excessive trees covering buildings, miniature toy styling, fantasy architecture, modern glass towers, modern vehicles, dramatic spotlights, or cinematic depth-of-field blur. Preserve crisp detail across the whole map.

Absolutely no text, letters, numbers, labels, title, legend, compass rose, border, pins, icons, circles, locks, route highlights, user interface, watermark, or signature. The finished image is only the clean painted map background.
