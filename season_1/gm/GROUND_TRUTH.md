# Season 1 ground truth

> STOP: GAME-MASTER SPOILERS. Players must not read this file before the final debrief.

This truth is immutable once play begins. Player theories, missed clues, or pacing pressure must never change the killer, method, motive, or timeline.

## Case identity

- **Campaign:** *Blackwater Static*
- **Town:** Hollowfield, Iowa, population 4,812
- **Date:** Friday, October 18, 1996
- **Victim:** Celia Wren, 38, investigative host of WOHF's late-night program *The Night Window*
- **Murderer:** Mayor June Kessler, 52
- **Actual murder site:** Blackwater Pump House
- **Body-staging site:** Saint Orra Packing House

## The murder

Celia Wren had reconstructed the truth behind Hollowfield's 1988 “Red Rain” disaster. As counsel for Saint Orra Packing, June Kessler ordered an illegal waste bypass opened during a governor's visit, then ordered a flood gate sealed when the discharge reacted with cleaning chemicals. Six night workers were trapped in a service tunnel and died in caustic vapor. June, Deputy Hollis Cade, and young emergency physician Miriam Vale helped falsify the public record. June later built her political career on “saving” Hollowfield when the plant closed.

Celia recovered the original red maintenance ledger and secretly recorded June admitting enough of the cover-up to destroy her. Celia and June had also been conducting a consensual affair in room 8 of the Northline Motor Lodge. June believed intimacy had bought Celia's silence. Celia intended to broadcast the confession at 10:00 p.m.

June asked Celia to meet at the Blackwater Pump House, claiming she would surrender the final municipal order. She dosed Celia's coffee with veterinary xylazine stolen earlier that afternoon from Bell Farm. When Celia weakened but refused to surrender the tape, June forced her face into the aeration basin until she drowned. Celia tore a gold-threaded button and fibers from June's ceremonial mayoral coat while fighting.

June wrapped the body in a blue municipal flood blanket, transported it in the town utility trailer, and staged it at the abandoned Saint Orra Packing House. She suspended Celia by the ankles on a kill rail, opened the abdomen with an old boning knife, removed the larynx, sealed both ears with red-and-gold municipal document wax, and fired an obsolete captive-bolt stunner into the skull after death. The tableau imitates the 1912 murder of labor organizer Ada Orra—the “Hush Woman”—and was meant to redirect suspicion toward people obsessed with packing-house history.

The display is savage, but forensic examination proves the mutilation and bolt wound were postmortem. Celia died by drowning in treated reservoir water.

## Proof standard

A defensible accusation requires all three of these independent chains:

1. **Cause and true scene:** Celia drowned in Blackwater water before her body was mutilated at Saint Orra.
2. **Movement and means:** June used her access code, civic Buick/trailer, stolen xylazine, municipal blanket, and packing-house key.
3. **Motive and consciousness of guilt:** the red ledger, affair evidence, recorded admission, damaged coat, or attempted suppression connects June to Celia's planned broadcast and the 1988 crimes.

One chain alone is insufficient. Two chains cause June to escalate. Three chains permit a final confrontation.

## Character truths

### June Kessler — murderer

Polished, intimate, and ferociously controlled. She admits the affair only when confronted with motel evidence. She blames the 1988 deaths on impossible choices. She initially claims she spent 6:40–7:40 alone drafting a festival speech at Town Hall. Her coat has a missing gold-threaded button, fresh tears at the cuff, and concealed scratches on her collarbone.

If confronted too early, she attacks the weakest proof and invokes civic stability. Once two proof chains converge, she destroys records and targets Gideon or Naomi. Once three converge, she tries to lure the investigators to Blackwater with the “final tape.”

### Celia Wren — victim

Brilliant, invasive, and willing to weaponize intimacy. She loved Naomi once and was having a consensual affair with June, partly sincere and partly investigative. She intended to expose June but also planned to broadcast private medical details without survivor consent. She is a victim, not a saint.

### Sheriff Hollis Cade

Not the murderer. In 1988 he moved bodies and altered the incident log on June's orders. Celia had threatened to expose him. At 6:50–7:42 he was at a domestic disturbance witnessed by four adults and recorded by dispatch. He tries to steer investigators toward an itinerant killer to protect the old cover-up.

### Dr. Miriam Vale

Not the murderer. As a young physician she falsified six death certificates after threats to close the only clinic. She preserved blood slides and water samples out of guilt. She recognizes xylazine and the drowning evidence but initially delays the full toxicology report.

### Naomi Venn

Not the murderer. Celia's producer and former lover. Naomi erased a personal reel containing an argument and explicit details of Celia's affair, fearing humiliation. Her live broadcast from 6:46–7:31 is independently archived.

### Gideon Rusk

Not the murderer. Former union steward and current packing-house caretaker. He gave Celia the red ledger. He saw the civic utility trailer leave Saint Orra but lies initially because June pays for his adult son's residential addiction treatment. He becomes the first secondary target.

### Vera Bell

Not the murderer. Diner owner. She saw June driving the civic Buick and trailer at 7:24. June previously prevented foreclosure on the diner, so Vera describes the driver only as “a woman” until pressed with empathy or proof.

### Dr. Amos Bell

Not the murderer. Veterinarian and Vera's brother. His missing xylazine creates a means-based red herring. He threatened Celia over her investigation into illegal livestock disposal and falsified treatment records. June was photographed near his medicine refrigerator at 4:18.

### Father Rowan Pike

Not the murderer. Keeper of Saint Leda's archive. He possesses the red ledger under the seal of confession and fears Celia's broadcast would expose surviving workers' medical and sexual histories. He knows the Hush Woman tableau and lied about his last meeting with Celia.

### Mae Orlov

Not the murderer. Owner of the Northline Motor Lodge. She photographed June and Celia together and attempted to extort June. She lies about room 8 and cleans it after every illicit meeting, but retains a Polaroid and guest slip.

### Silas Dredge

Not the murderer. Mortician and amateur local historian. His anatomical skill, antique tools, and fixation on Ada Orra make him an intentional red herring. Tool marks show the mutilation was performed by someone accustomed to meat cutting, not surgery or embalming.
